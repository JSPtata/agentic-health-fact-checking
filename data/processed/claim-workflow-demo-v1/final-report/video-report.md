## fitness-02-c002

Avoid drinking coffee on an empty stomach to prevent acidity.

Disposition: adjudicated

Medical verdict: insufficient_evidence

Method: assisted

In the two appraised abstracts, a review reports coffee-related gastric acid secretion and reflux, while a small before-and-after study measures sucrose permeability. Neither abstract establishes that avoiding coffee specifically on an empty stomach prevents a defined acid-related clinical outcome. This is an assisted, abstract-only assessment of a bounded selection, not a finding that coffee has no physiological effects or that the claim is false.

- Define the intended acidity endpoint without treating acid secretion, reflux, heartburn and dyspepsia as interchangeable.
- A controlled comparison of fasting coffee with an appropriate alternative, measuring the intended preventive outcome, is not reported in the appraised abstracts.
- Contemporary evidence, population applicability, dose and coffee preparation, clinical importance, and study-level risk of bias have not been systematically assessed.
- Assisted appraisal of 2/3 candidate abstracts. Other candidates were not appraised in this assessment. No full-text, exhaustive-search or systematic risk-of-bias appraisal was performed.

## fitness-02-c006

Maintain proper posture while sitting to prevent back pain.

Disposition: adjudicated

Medical verdict: insufficient_evidence

Method: assisted

The two appraised abstracts concern treatment in people already experiencing low back pain and mixed review-level evidence about physical exposure and causality. They do not establish that an isolated, defined proper sitting posture prevents back-pain onset or recurrence. This bounded assisted assessment does not establish that posture is irrelevant or ineffective.

- Define the proposed sitting posture and intended prevention population, including whether onset or recurrence is the target.
- An isolated sitting-posture intervention with incident or recurrent back-pain outcomes is not established by the appraised trial abstract.
- Confounding, mixed interventions, review overlap, risk of bias, effect sizes and clinical importance require further appraisal.
- Assisted appraisal of 2/77 candidate abstracts. Other candidates were not appraised in this assessment. No full-text, exhaustive-search or systematic risk-of-bias appraisal was performed.
