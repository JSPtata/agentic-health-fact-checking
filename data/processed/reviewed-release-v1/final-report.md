## fitness-02-c001

Always stretch your body after waking up to boost circulation.

Result: insufficient_evidence

Review: assisted_legacy

Stretching can produce local vascular responses under particular conditions, but the supplied evidence does not establish that everyone should always stretch after waking to boost circulation. Missing direct support is not proof that the claim is false.


## fitness-02-c002

Avoid drinking coffee on an empty stomach to prevent acidity.

Result: insufficient_evidence

Review: assisted

In the two appraised abstracts, a review reports coffee-related gastric acid secretion and reflux, while a small before-and-after study measures sucrose permeability. Neither abstract establishes that avoiding coffee specifically on an empty stomach prevents a defined acid-related clinical outcome. This is an assisted, abstract-only assessment of a bounded selection, not a finding that coffee has no physiological effects or that the claim is false.

- Avoid drinking coffee on an empty stomach to prevent acidity. — insufficient_evidence
  - PMID:10499460: "Coffee stimulates gastrin release and gastric acid secretion, but studies on the effect on lower oesophageal sphincter pressure yield conflicting results."
  - PMID:15841908: "The probands underwent SaLM test twice--first time without coffee restriction and second time after 48-hour coffee abstinence."

## fitness-02-c003

Eat fruits before meals, not after, for better digestion.

Result: insufficient_evidence

Review: assisted

The two appraised abstracts describe kiwifruit-related digestive benefits and baobab-extract experiments, but do not report a comparison of eating whole fruits before versus after meals on a defined human digestive outcome. This bounded assisted assessment cannot establish the timing recommendation or refute digestive benefits of particular fruits.

- Eat fruits before meals, not after, for better digestion. — insufficient_evidence
  - PMID:23746068: "Consuming gold kiwifruit with iron-rich meals improves poor iron status, and green kiwifruit aids digestion and laxation."
  - PMID:24176228: "In vivo, baobab extract was consumed in solution at both a low-dose (18.5 g) and a high-dose (37 g) aqueous drink in 250 mL of water along with white bread, and resulting GR, satiety, and postprandial energy expenditure were measured."

## fitness-02-c006

Maintain proper posture while sitting to prevent back pain.

Result: insufficient_evidence

Review: assisted

The two appraised abstracts concern treatment in people already experiencing low back pain and mixed review-level evidence about physical exposure and causality. They do not establish that an isolated, defined proper sitting posture prevents back-pain onset or recurrence. This bounded assisted assessment does not establish that posture is irrelevant or ineffective.

- Maintain proper posture while sitting to prevent back pain. — insufficient_evidence
  - PMID:34306274: "The purpose of this study was to investigate the effects of multidimensional approach model on the pain, disability, and sitting posture in patients with nonspecific low back pain (LBP)."
  - PMID:31451200: "Evidence that these factors precede first time LBP or have a dose response relationship with LBP outcomes was mixed."

## nutrition-01-diagnostic-c001

People previously eating a lot of processed foods and excess carbohydrates and fats together lose a lot of fat very quickly when they switch to eating only meat, fish, eggs, vegetables and fruit, with water, coffee or tea and without alcohol or milk.

Result: insufficient_evidence

Review: assisted

Within the reviewed 17-abstract development pool, related studies report body-fat changes, weight changes or dietary associations, but the evidence does not establish or refute the exact restricted-food claim and its large, rapid fat-loss wording. Weight change is not equivalent to fat loss; adherence associations are not randomized dietary effects. Guidance does not experimentally refute this dietary package. Magnitude and timing remain unspecified. This completed assisted assessment is limited to the supplied abstracts and is not an exhaustive or independently validated verification of the claim.

- Switching to the exact foods and beverages described in the claim, including the stated exclusions, produces body-fat loss. — insufficient_evidence
  - PMID:26278052: "Body fat loss was calculated as the difference between daily fat intake and net fat oxidation measured while residing in a metabolic chamber."
  - PMID:33802738: "Participants were asked to follow a Paleolithic diet for 12 weeks and were randomized to supervised exercise or general exercise recommendations."
  - PMID:40760353: "The primary outcome was the within-participant difference in percent weight change (%WC) between diets, from baseline to week 8."
  - PMID:26278052: "Whereas carbohydrate restriction led to sustained increases in fat oxidation and loss of 53 ± 6 g/day of body fat, fat oxidation was unchanged by fat restriction, leading to 89 ± 6 g/day of fat loss, and was significantly greater than carbohydrate restriction (p = 0.002)."
  - PMID:33802738: "A higher Paleo ratio was associated with lower fat mass, BMI, waist circumference, systolic blood pressure, and serum triglycerides at 12 weeks, but not with lower HbA1c levels."
- The amount of body-fat loss is large, as expressed by "a lot". — insufficient_evidence
  - PMID:25178568: "fat mass (mean difference in change, -1.5% [CI, -2.6% to -0.4%]; P = 0.011)"
  - PMID:25178568: "At 12 months, participants on the low-carbohydrate diet had greater decreases in weight (mean difference in change, -3.5 kg [95% CI, -5.6 to -1.4 kg]; P = 0.002), fat mass (mean difference in change, -1.5% [CI, -2.6% to -0.4%]; P = 0.011)"
- Body-fat loss occurs "very quickly". — insufficient_evidence
  - PMID:26278052: "We selectively restricted dietary carbohydrate versus fat for 6 days following a 5-day baseline diet in 19 adults with obesity confined to a metabolic ward where they exercised daily."
  - PMID:25178568: "Data on weight, cardiovascular risk factors, and dietary composition were collected at 0, 3, 6, and 12 months."
- The effect applies to people previously eating a lot of processed foods and excess carbohydrates and fats together. — insufficient_evidence
  - PMID:40760353: "habitual UPF intake ≥50% kcal day-1"
  - PMID:33802738: "individuals with type 2 diabetes"
  - PMID:40760353: "55 adults in England (body mass index ≥25 to <40 kg m-2, habitual UPF intake ≥50% kcal day-1) were provided with two 8-week ad libitum diets following the UK Eatwell Guide"
